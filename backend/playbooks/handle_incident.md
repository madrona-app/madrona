# Handle an Incident

Use this when something has gone wrong — an object has been damaged, lost, stolen, dropped, exposed to water or fire, infested by pests, vandalised, or otherwise affected by an event that compromises it. Incident reports are a legal record and the trigger for insurance, conservation, and (sometimes) law-enforcement workflows.

The single most common incident scenario is **new damage noticed when a loan returns** — a fresh crack on a frame, a scratch on a case, a chip on a ceramic, a tear in a textile — compared to the pre-loan condition record. If you notice anything new on a returning loan object, that is an incident and needs to be documented here, not just in the condition report. The condition report captures the current state; the incident captures the event that changed it.

## Where to go in Madrona

**Collections → Care & Risk → Incidents** and press **New incident**. If the incident affects a specific object, start from its workspace via the **Procedures** tab so the link is automatic.

## Priority: act first, document soon after

For active incidents — a fire, a flood, a theft in progress, a pest outbreak — the first thing is containment and response per your emergency plan. Documentation follows as soon as the situation is stable. Don't let form-filling delay emergency response.

That said: start the incident record the *same day*. Memories fade, facts get muddled, and insurance claims hinge on precise timelines.

## Damage discovered on loan return

When a loan comes back and you notice new damage during post-loan inspection, follow this sequence:

1. **Stop handling the object.** Leave it where the damage was discovered and photograph it before moving it. The position and orientation at discovery matters.
2. **Open an incident record.** Incident type: damage. Discovered by: you. Discovery date: today. Description: what you found, factually — "a new crack on the upper-left corner of the frame, running 4 cm diagonally, not present in the pre-loan condition photos." No speculation about when or how it happened.
3. **File a return condition report** that explicitly references the pre-loan baseline. Link the report to the incident.
4. **Notify the borrower immediately.** Their insurance may be on the hook; the loan agreement specifies the notification window (usually 24–72 hours). Do not clean, repair, or alter the object before the borrower has been notified.
5. **Conservator review.** The conservator decides whether treatment is needed and what kind. Open a conservation treatment record if they recommend one.
6. **Keep the loan record open** until the incident is fully resolved — insurance, treatment, and borrower sign-off. Closing the loan early locks the damage story in a way that makes claims harder.

This is the loan-return workflow. Use it for any "we found something new" moment: a new crack, a new scratch, a new chip, a new tear, a loose part, or a stain that wasn't there before.

## What to record

1. **Incident type** — damage, loss, theft, environmental event, pest, vandalism, handling accident, other.
2. **Incident date** — when the incident actually happened (or was discovered, if you don't know when).
3. **Discovered by and discovery date** — who first noticed, and when.
4. **Location** — where the incident occurred.
5. **Affected objects** — each object affected, linked via the record linker. For large-scale events (flood, fire), you may add objects incrementally as you assess the damage.
6. **Description** — what happened, in factual clinical terms. Don't speculate about cause or blame.
7. **Initial response** — what was done immediately (object moved to dry area, pests isolated, police called, etc.).

## Assessment and follow-up

For each affected object:

- Open a condition report documenting the damage state
- If the object needs stabilisation or repair, open a conservation treatment
- If the damage is significant, trigger the insurance notification workflow per your policy

## Legal and reporting

- **Theft or suspected theft** — notify law enforcement, your insurance carrier, and (for cultural property) relevant registries.
- **NAGPRA items** — damage or loss of items with NAGPRA status may trigger specific notification requirements to the affiliated tribe or nation.
- **Loaned items** — if the damaged object is on loan to or from another institution, the lender must be notified immediately. The loan agreement governs the exact timeline.
- **Major incidents** — your emergency plan should name an institutional spokesperson; don't speak to press or make public statements without coordination.

## Closing the incident

An incident is closed when:

- All affected objects have been assessed (condition reports filed)
- Any required treatment has been started or completed
- Insurance claims and external notifications are done
- A post-incident review captures what was learned

Do not close the incident earlier just because the immediate response is over. An incident with outstanding conservation or insurance actions stays open.

## Common pitfalls

- Writing speculation instead of facts. "The handler dropped it" without evidence is both wrong (might not be accurate) and dangerous (legally actionable). "Object found on floor at 9am" is safe.
- Delaying the record until "after things settle". Details will be wrong by then.
- Not linking affected objects to the incident, so there's no way to find the incident later by searching for the object.
- Closing the record before the downstream actions are done.

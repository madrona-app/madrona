# Record a Valuation

Use this when you need to document the monetary value of an object — for insurance coverage, indemnity applications, loan agreements, donor tax receipts, probate, or internal collection assessment. Valuations are not permanent; they're snapshots that must be updated periodically and whenever circumstances change.

## Where to go in Madrona

**Collections → Care & Risk → Valuations** and press **New valuation**. You can also start one from an object's workspace via the **Procedures** tab or the ellipsis menu.

## Types of valuation

Choose the right type — each serves a different purpose and may use a different methodology:

- **Insurance** — the replacement value for insurance purposes. Usually the fair market value plus any costs to replace or restore. Required for any object in transit or on display.
- **Indemnity** — the value declared for government indemnity schemes. Often the same as insurance value but may have its own rules depending on the scheme.
- **Market** — current fair market value. What the object would fetch at auction or in a private sale today.
- **Donation** — the appraised value for donor tax deductions. In the US, donations over $5,000 require a qualified independent appraisal (IRS Form 8283). The museum must not provide or influence this valuation.
- **Probate** — value for estate settlement.
- **Internal** — your institution's own assessment for planning, prioritisation, or salvage triage. Not for external use.

## What to record

1. **Object** — the specific object being valued.
2. **Valuation type** — one of the types above.
3. **Amount and currency** — the value and its currency. For collections with international objects, be explicit about currency.
4. **Valuation date** — when this valuation was determined.
5. **Valued by** — who performed or commissioned the valuation. For insurance and donation valuations, this should be a qualified appraiser, not museum staff.
6. **Methodology** — how the value was determined: comparable sales, replacement cost, expert opinion, auction estimate, etc.
7. **Valid until** — when this valuation expires. Insurance valuations typically need refreshing every 3-5 years; loan valuations at the start of each loan period.

## Procedure context

Valuation is part of the broader Insurance and Indemnity Management procedure, which requires that you maintain up-to-date valuations for all objects that are insured, indemnified, or in transit. A stale valuation puts both the institution and the insurer at risk — undervaluation means inadequate coverage, overvaluation means inflated premiums.

## When to revalue

- Before any loan (in or out) — the loan agreement needs a current value
- After conservation treatment — the treatment may have increased or decreased the value
- After a market shift — if comparable objects have sold at significantly different prices
- When the insurance policy renews — verify the total insured value still covers the collection
- After damage — even if repaired, the value may have changed

## Common pitfalls

- Using an old valuation for a new loan. Lenders and borrowers both need current numbers.
- Museum staff providing donation valuations. This is an IRS violation for US institutions — the donor must obtain an independent appraisal.
- Recording a valuation without the methodology. Future staff need to know how the number was derived to decide if it's still valid.
- Not tracking expiry dates. A 10-year-old insurance valuation is almost certainly wrong.

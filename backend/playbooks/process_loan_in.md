# Process an Incoming Loan

Use this when another institution is lending you objects — for an exhibition you're hosting, a research project, a conservation collaboration, or a study visit. This playbook assumes you are the *borrower*.

## Where to go in Madrona

**Collections → Transactions → Loans In** and press **New loan**. If the objects arrived first and you already created an object entry, start from there — Madrona will pre-fill the lender and entry reference.

## The procedure phases

A loan in has four phases and Madrona enforces them as workflow statuses. You don't move the loan forward until each phase is complete.

1. **Proposal** — you know who the lender is, roughly what they're lending, and what you want it for.
2. **Agreement** — the lender has formally authorized the loan; you've signed the loan agreement; insurance and shipping are arranged.
3. **Active** — the objects are in your care. You're responsible for them.
4. **Returned** — the objects have gone back, condition confirmed, loan closed.

## Phase 1: proposal

Fill in the lender (as a constituent), the loan purpose, the intended start and end dates, and a list of the requested objects. For each object, record the lender's insurance value — this drives your coverage requirements.

Don't skip the **purpose** field. Some lenders require specific purposes be named (exhibition X, research project Y), and "general display" won't cut it for a serious lender.

## Phase 2: agreement

This is where most loans get stuck. The requirements Madrona enforces here:

- **Lender authorizer** — a named person at the lending institution who has formal authority to approve the loan. Not the shipping coordinator, not the registrar — the person whose signature is on the authorization.
- **Loan agreement reference** — the document number on your signed loan agreement. Both parties retain signed copies.
- **Insurance arrangement** — who's covering the objects, at what value, from door to door or at-the-nail-to-at-the-nail. Document clearly.
- **Facilities report** — lenders almost always want a facilities report describing your venue, security, climate, and handling practices. Record when you sent it and when the lender approved it.
- **Handling and installation plan** — how the objects will be handled, displayed, and stored while on loan.

Once all four requirements are green, the loan can advance to active.

## Phase 3: active

The objects are in your custody. Your obligations:

- Periodic condition checks — frequency depends on the loan agreement and object type
- Environmental monitoring — keep it within the agreed conditions
- No movements or activities outside what the agreement allows without written lender approval
- Incident reporting — any damage, theft, or concern is reported to the lender immediately

## Phase 4: return

When the loan period ends:

- File a final condition report comparing object state to the intake
- Arrange the return shipping per the agreement
- Get lender acknowledgment of receipt
- Close the loan in Madrona — this archives the record

## Common pitfalls

- Starting phase 2 without a named lender authorizer. The loan will sit blocked until you get the name in writing.
- Forgetting the facilities report for a lender who asked for one. Send it early; approvals take weeks.
- Treating the loan as "done" when the objects leave — the record isn't closed until the lender confirms receipt.

# Start a Conservation Treatment

Use this when an object needs active intervention from a conservator — cleaning, stabilisation, repair, mount-making, reframing, backing replacement, and so on. This playbook covers the workflow from "the object needs work" through the completed treatment record.

## Where to go in Madrona

**Collections → Care & Risk → Conservation** and press **New treatment**. You can also start one from the object's workspace via the **Procedures** tab.

## The procedure phases

Madrona's conservation workflow follows three phases:

1. **Agreement** — the decision to treat, the conservator, the scope, and the authorization.
2. **Execution** — the work itself, materials used, dates, and any photographs.
3. **Outcome** — what actually happened, recommendations, future care notes, any condition change.

## Phase 1: agreement

Before anyone touches the object:

- **Treatment type** — cleaning, structural stabilisation, reframing, mount work, inpainting, etc. Drives reporting.
- **Object** — link to the specific object.
- **Conservator** — who is doing the work. Mark the *is external* flag if this is an outside conservator, not your in-house team.
- **Proposed by** — who flagged that the object needs treatment (a curator, registrar, or your own condition check).
- **Proposal date** — when the proposal was made.
- **Treatment rationale** — *why* this work needs to happen. Be specific: "cleaning to remove accumulated surface grime obscuring the signature" not "cleaning".
- **Estimated cost and duration** — rough numbers. Used for budgeting and reporting.
- **Authorization** — approval note from whoever has authority to approve conservation work at your institution. This is often the senior conservator, the registrar, or the curator of the relevant collection.

Ethical note: every treatment is a permanent change to the object. Treatment authorization is non-negotiable. Don't start until it's on paper.

## Phase 2: execution

During the treatment:

- **Dates** — start and end.
- **Description and methods** — what was actually done. This is the core record that future conservators will read when deciding on any next treatment.
- **Materials used** — add each material via the materials section. Use controlled vocabulary. Include quantities where relevant.
- **Photographs** — before, during, and after. Media section with usage type set to the appropriate phase.
- **External treatment details** — if the object left your institution, record the dispatch date, return date, shipping arrangements, and insurance value during transit.

## Phase 3: outcome

After the work:

- **Actual cost and duration** — what it actually took. Useful for the next estimate.
- **Before / after condition reports** — link the two condition reports. The comparison *is* the treatment record's proof of change.
- **Recommendations** — future care, handling, environmental conditions, any monitoring needs.
- **Restrictions** — display duration limits, light level caps, handling constraints.
- **Recall date** — when the object should be re-examined or re-treated, if applicable.
- **Treatment note** — the clinical summary of the outcome.
- **Internal note** — any conservator-to-conservator observations that aren't appropriate for public reporting.

## Common pitfalls

- Starting work before authorization is recorded. Even if the conservator "knows" they're cleared, the paper trail is the legal record.
- Missing materials. A treatment record with just "cleaned" as the methods text is not useful five years later when someone wants to re-treat.
- No before/after photos. The whole point of the record is the comparison.
- Not linking the before and after condition reports. They belong on the treatment record so the change is documented in one place.

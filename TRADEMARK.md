What the licence does not give you is the name. Apache-2.0 section 6 says so
directly: the licence "does not grant permission to use the trade names,
trademarks, service marks, or product names of the Licensor". This document

- Run, modify, fork, or redistribute the software, including commercially.
- Say truthfully what your project is or is built on: "a fork of Madrona",

  "we migrated from Madrona".
- Offer installation, hosting, support, training, or consulting **for Madrona**,
  and say that is what you do.
- Use the name in articles, talks, documentation, comparisons, reviews, course
  material, academic work, and job listings.
- Keep the existing copyright and NOTICE attributions in a fork. You are

This is ordinary descriptive use. We are not going to come after you for
accurately describing what your software is.

- Name a product, service, company, or domain "Madrona", or include "Madrona"

## The distinction in one line

Describing a relationship to Madrona is fine. Adopting the identity is not.

"Acme DAM, powered by Madrona" needs no permission. "Acme Madrona" does.

## Modified versions

If you distribute a modified version, please make it clear that it is yours and
not ours — a different product name, and a note in your README that it is a
modified version. This protects users from filing bugs with us that we cannot
reproduce, and protects you from being blamed for defects in our code.

The logo and wordmark should be replaced in a redistributed fork that carries
its own name. The `docs/assets/` artwork is ours; the rest of the repository is
yours to use under Apache-2.0.

## Asking

Requests, edge cases, and anything this document does not obviously cover:
open an issue or write to us. We would rather answer a question than discover a
misunderstanding later. Permission, when granted, is in writing and specific —
it is not implied by our silence, by a merged pull request, or by our having
seen your project and said nothing.
